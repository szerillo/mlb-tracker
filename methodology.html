How the Playoff Futures models work

Three ways to price October. Pick one on the board, or compare them side by side.

Every number is a probability: a team's chance to reach a round, win the pennant, or win the World Series. Three engines produce those numbers, each answering the question a little differently.

Game Sim The bracket, played out

Takes the actual playoff bracket and simulates it game by game, thousands of times, using the same game projection model that powers my Action PRO odds. For these speculative playoff games it assumes neutral weather and umpire effects and a rested bullpen for both teams.

Playoff Fit Built for October

My October-specific team model. It scores how each roster and pitching staff fits tournament baseball, calibrated on past postseasons and validated against real playoff series results and market prices. I screened a large set of candidate metrics and narrowed them to the four components that have most driven playoff success:

  • Lineup quality, lineup xwOBA
  • Lineup contact, strikeout avoidance (K%)
  • Team defense, Outs Above Average
  • Starting rotation, October-weighted rotation strength

Composite The wisdom of the crowd

An equal-weight blend of several independent forecasts (PECOTA, FanGraphs, ATC, THE BAT, OOPSY) alongside my own Game Sim and Playoff Fit projections. No single model dominates; the blend smooths out any one system's blind spot, which has proven the most reliable way to calibrate preseason win-total projections. If you only want one column, this is the safest default.

Reading the Edges tab

The Edges tab lines each model up against the betting market, per team:

ListedThe best available sportsbook price for that team. Mkt%That price converted to an implied probability. Model %The selected model's probability for that team. FairThe model's probability converted back into American odds, the price we'd call fair. EdgeModel % minus market %. Positive means the model likes the team more than the market. ★ marks the bigger gaps.

Raw vs Devig. A sportsbook builds a margin (the "vig") into its prices, so the implied odds across a whole field add up to more than 100%. Raw shows those book-implied percentages as-is. Devig strips the margin out. It normalizes the field back to 100% (per league for the pennant) so you see the market's true implied probability. Compare your edge against Devig for the cleaner read; Raw shows what's actually posted.

Probabilities update through the day as lineups, rotations, and prices move. Game Sim and Playoff Fit are logged side by side all October and graded against results; over time the ledger, not the label, decides which to trust.
