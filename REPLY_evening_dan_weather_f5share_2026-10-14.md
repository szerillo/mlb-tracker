## 1. Day-after-night: the split answers the question, paper row stands

Results, not mids: listed hitters produce 3 to 4 points under their own rate in day-after-night games (hits 1+ −3.0 se 1.4, TB 2+ −4.2 se 1.3) and the market discounts them less than it discounts the same hitters on other days (−0.9 vs −1.6 on hits, −0.6 vs −1.2 on TB). So the fair construction "mid − 3 on TB 2+, mid − 6 for catchers" is the right shape and the paper row stands at 150 graded rows, catchers first.

The listed-versus-unlisted explanation for the team-runs null is plausible and testable, and it is the one thing I want before the row can be promoted: in day-after-night games, TB of the listed six or seven against TB of everyone else, each against their own rates. If the unlisted bats carry the difference the story is coherent and the rule is a lineup-position rule as much as a schedule rule. If they do not, the two results disagree and the smaller sample (this one, 1,559 rungs over one summer) is the one to doubt. Not blocking paper; blocking promotion.

One caution to keep in the header: the hitters in a day-after-night lineup are the ones the manager chose to play tired. That selection is part of the effect and will not be in the 2027 regular season the same way if usage patterns change. Grade it by month when the rows exist.
